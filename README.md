This repository contains the data and related coding for my analysis of the drought resilience of Renosterveld in Drie Kuilen. 

Due to anthropogenic activities, climate conditions are becoming more intense. This has the effect of increasing the intensity and frequency of extreme weather conditions such as drought and increasing rainfall variability. Given the changing conditions, it is essential to understand how it affects plant life in order to better conserve and protect it. Renosterveld habitats have become endangered due to anthropogenic land use and habitat destruction. This experiment aims to test the drought resilience of Renosterveld, a keystone vegetation type in the GCFR. 12 Plots were set up in the Drie Kuilen Nature Reserve in Western Cape, South Africa. Six of the plots simulated drought conditions for the plants within them while the other six plots were under environmental conditions. Sap flow sensors were attached to at least one plant per plot together with a thermocouple, measuring sap flow within the plant. This data was used to compare the plants response to drought and compare it environmental conditions, using heat ratio as a measure of sap flow and transpiration. The data suggested that Renosterveld had a plastic response to drought, closing its stomata in times of water scarcity to conserve resources, shown through a reduced level of sap flow. It has adapted to this in a
variety of ways, however the future for renosterveld is uncertain given the lack of long-term data on their response to the warming climate.

Variables include:
Heat ratio as a measure of sapflow: measures transpiration under both drought and environmental conditions.
VPD (Vapour Pressure Deficit): the difference between the moisture in the air and how much moisture the air could hold when saturated
Soil Moisture: measures moisture in the soil
Temp: measures max temperature throughout the day in celcius
